## 对话（Dialogue）

**Lucy:** I'm hungry. What would you like for dinner?

**Tom:** Let's order something warm. I'd like a bowl of beef noodles.

**Lucy:** I think I'll have a sandwich and some carrot soup.

**Tom:** On cold days, I love porridge with a little sugar. It's simple but delicious.

**Lucy:** Food is not just for eating. It has a special connection to our culture.

**Tom:** You're right. Every dish tells a story about the people who make it.

**Lucy:** Then let's try something new and discover the story behind it.

## 短文（Reading）

### Food Matters

Everyone needs food. It gives us energy and keeps us healthy. But food is more than fuel. It is a part of our homes, our memories and our culture.

Different places have different foods. In the north, people like sweet and strong flavours - beef, mutton and pies are popular in winter. In the south, people might choose lighter meals with rice and fresh vegetables. No matter where we are, the food we grow up with often becomes our favourite, and it reminds us of home.

The way we cook is also very important. A pot of soup can take a whole afternoon. A simple pancake can be made in minutes. We scramble eggs in the morning, slice an onion, and fry it with carrots. These small actions are part of a daily family life.

But we must be careful, too. Too much fat or too much sugar is not good for us. Some people treat food like medicine, taking a pill when they feel ill. Others eat all day and forget to rest. A healthy meal should be plain but good, with enough vegetables and not too much salt.

Food also brings people together. At a family dinner, we sit around the table and share what happens in our lives. A certain dish can make us remember an old story, and a shared meal can prove how much we care about each other. Some traditions remain the same for hundreds of years, while others change as people travel and learn from one another.

Next time you sit down to eat, think about the food on your plate. It is connected to the earth, to the farmers, to your family and to you. Food matters more than we think.
